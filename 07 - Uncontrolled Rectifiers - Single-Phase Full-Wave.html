What to notice now

For an ideal resistive load, the output is |v_s| and repeats twice per source period. The source current remains alternating even though the load current is unidirectional.

Guided investigations

01

Predict the second half-cycle

At θ = 225°, predict the signs of v_s, v_o, i_o and i_s, and which diode pair conducts.

Reveal the explanation
The source is negative, but D3–D4 reverse its connection to the load. Therefore v_o and i_o remain positive while i_s is negative.
02

Compare R and RL

Select RL and increase L. Predict what changes strongly and what remains imposed by the bridge.

Reveal the explanation
Inductance smooths i_o. With an ideal bridge and E = 0, v_o still follows |v_s|; the bridge pair commutates each half-cycle.

03

Count the diode drops

Enable V_F and compare the output with the half-wave circuit. How many forward drops are in the active bridge path?

Reveal the explanation
A bridge source path contains two conducting diodes, so the available rectified source voltage is |v_s| − 2V_F while that pair conducts.
04

Add a capacitor filter

Choose R‖C and increase C. Predict the output-ripple frequency and the change in source-current pulses.

Reveal the explanation
The capacitor is refreshed near both source-voltage peaks, so ripple occurs mainly at 2f. Larger C reduces voltage ripple but concentrates source current into shorter charging intervals.

Model and equations

For R/RL/RLE, the bridge delivers a rectified source through two diodes. When current is zero, conduction starts only when the available rectified source exceeds E.

vr(t) = |Vm sin(ωt)| − 2VF
conducting series load: Ldio/dt + Rio + E = vr

For R‖C, the capacitor charges when the available bridge voltage exceeds its present voltage and otherwise discharges through R. The ideal source and zero source impedance make the charging-current peaks intentionally idealized.

Bridge versus center-tapped full wave

  • Bridge: four diodes, two conduct at a time.
  • Center-tapped: two diodes, one conducts at a time, but it needs a center-tapped secondary.
  • For the same defined source winding voltage, diode reverse-voltage requirements differ; always state the voltage reference before comparing.

What changed from half wave?

  • Both source half-cycles transfer energy to the load.
  • The ideal resistive output average rises from V_m/π to 2V_m/π.
  • Output ripple repeats at 2f rather than f.
  • Source current remains AC and changes sign every half-cycle.
Definitions, conventions, and scope

Source reference: v_s = V_m sin(ωt). Positive load current enters the positive output terminal. D1–D2 conduct for positive source polarity; D3–D4 conduct for negative source polarity. All averages are over one source period.

The capacitor-filter model omits E and L. The series-load model omits C. The source has zero internal impedance and zero commutation inductance, so commutation is instantaneous and capacitor charging peaks are idealized.

Diode forward voltage is constant when enabled. Reverse recovery, switching losses, temperature dependence, transformer copper/core loss and source impedance are not modeled.
